( about ) About Policy Fit Check

Policy Fit Check exists because insurance is confusing on purpose, or at least it can feel that way. Different providers, different fine print, different words for the same thing, and a hundred small conditions buried in a PDF nobody reads.

We built a company whose only job is to cut through that. We compare real policies from real providers and match you to the ones that actually fit your life: your age, your circumstances, your budget, the things you actually need covered.

We're not trying to sell you a policy. We're trying to help you understand one.

That's the difference. A comparison site shows you a list. We try to explain why one option on that list is right for you, and why the others aren't, in plain language, before you commit to anything.

A short story: why this exists

A friend of Onyinyechi's, a nurse in her mid-twenties, once bought income protection because a colleague recommended it. She never read past the first page. Two years later, she needed to make a claim, and found out the policy excluded exactly the kind of injury she'd had, a detail that had been sitting in the small print the entire time.

Nothing about what she did was careless. She did what almost everyone does: she trusted that a sensible-sounding policy would sensibly cover her. It's the small print that let her down, not her judgement.

That's the gap Policy Fit Check is built to close. Not by making insurance simple to the point of leaving things out, but by making sure the fit is right before the signature happens, and that you actually know what you're signing.

Our Mission

To make insurance simpler, more transparent, and more personal, by helping people find the policy that actually fits who they are.

Our Vision

To become the place people trust when they need to make an insurance decision: somewhere you can compare your options, understand what you're looking at, and choose with confidence instead of guesswork.

( cover list ) Insurance, read properly

The catalog only makes sense if the underlying products do. Here is how real insurers approach each major type of cover, paraphrased from how the market works — the actual policies PFC matches against are the live catalog in the pipeline, not this page.

Life insurance

Life cover pays a lump sum if the insured person dies during the term. Insurers such as Aviva, Legal & General and Zurich typically offer level term cover (the sum stays flat) and decreasing term (commonly mortgage-linked), and almost all exclude suicide within roughly the first 12–13 months of the policy. Premiums rise with age and health risk; a waiting period before a claim is normal.

Health insurance

Private medical insurance pays for private hospital treatment and, on richer plans, day-to-day care like GP visits and dental. In Ireland the big names are Vhi, Laya and Irish Life Health; in the UK, Bupa is a common example. Nearly every health plan applies waiting periods for new or pre-existing conditions, and some insurers now include mental-health support packages rather than excluding them outright.

Income protection

Income protection replaces part of your salary if illness or injury stops you working. Insurers such as Aviva, Royal London and Legal & General use a deferred (waiting) period, commonly between 4 and 13 weeks, before payments start. The definition matters enormously: "own-occupation" pays if you cannot do your own job, while "any-occupation" only pays if you cannot do any job.

Home & renters insurance

Buildings cover protects the structure, contents cover protects what is inside it, and you can usually buy either separately. Insurers like AXA, Zurich and Aviva commonly exclude wear and tear and policies often go unoccupied for more than 60 days. Renters rarely need buildings cover, which is why the catalog carries contents-only options.

Travel insurance

Travel cover splits into single-trip and annual multi-trip, with medical bills, cancellation and baggage. Providers such as Allianz and AXA routinely exclude travel against government advisories and typically ask you to declare pre-existing conditions rather than quietly rejecting them. Medical cover limits on cheaper plans can be dramatically lower than on premium ones.

Auto insurance

Motor cover runs from third-party only up to fully comprehensive, with optional extras like breakdown cover and no-claims protection. Insurers such as Aviva, AXA and Allianz treat driving under the influence and racing or track-day use as voiding claims, and many now offer telematics (black-box) discounts to younger drivers.

Critical illness

Critical illness pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a listed condition, typically 40 or more. Most policies apply a survival period (commonly around 27 days) and exclude conditions diagnosed before the policy started. Because it pays on diagnosis rather than on death, it is often paired with life cover rather than replacing it.

Pet insurance

Pet cover pays vets' bills, usually with a choice between lifetime cover, 12-month time-limited cover, and accident-only. The market-wide catch is that pre-existing conditions and routine or preventative care are almost always excluded, and lifetime plans cap what they pay per condition each year.

Student cover

Student products bundle the things young renters and travellers actually need: contents cover for shared accommodation, low-cost health cover for under-26s, and study-abroad travel add-ons. The honest catch is that theft without forced entry and undeclared high-value items are typically excluded, which matters in shared houses.

Business liability

Business liability covers claims against a company, including employers' liability (statutory in most places), public liability, and professional indemnity. Insurers such as Allianz and Aviva tend to exclude contractual liability beyond common law and deliberate acts, and cyber liability is now commonly sold as an add-on.

( pipeline ) How the five agents work

A small organisation, visible while it works. Click any agent to meet it, address it in the chat below, or run a real match above and watch the handoffs happen for real.

  1. Nadia Researcher

    Queries the live policy catalog at the moment you ask, filters it against your age band and life stage, and names the gaps honestly.

  2. Milo Designer

    Decides how the candidates should be compared, and scores every eligible policy against what actually matters for your profile.

  3. Priya Maker

    Builds your shortlist from the real catalog fields: name, provider, premium, coverage. Nothing is invented; if the data is missing, she says so.

  4. Sasha Communicator

    Writes the one-line reason under each policy in plain language, grounded in the actual numbers and the actual fine print.

  5. Callum Manager

    Reviews the handoffs, keeps the honest empty state visible when the catalog has nothing good, and synthesises the final recommendation.

This page runs on a static site with no backend. The agents' reasoning is deterministic and grounded in the live catalog, so the demo always works; the optional live-LLM variant is documented in the repo.

( principles ) The UI/UX principles this site is built on

Visibility of system status

The pipeline is never decorative: nodes show idle, working and done states that track real execution, and a live log reports what actually happened with the real catalog.

Honest language

No fake matches, no padded empty states, no invented data. When the catalog is thin, the site says so in plain words.

Progressive disclosure

Six questions, one at a time. The match runs only when enough is known to run it well.

Recognition over recall

Labels say "Senior" not "life stage category 07". The agents have names, faces and roles before they are expected to be trusted.

Consistency

One token system — ink, parchment, brass, signal — and one type pairing across every section, so motion never has to carry branding.

Accessibility

Keyboard focus is visible everywhere, reduced-motion users get a stepped indicator instead of the pulse, and every interaction has a non-motion fallback.

Feedback and affordance

Buttons always do something, chips are obviously chips, and every agent node is a button you can actually press to meet the agent.

Responsive by default

Everything degrades gracefully to one column and a stacked pipeline on a phone, not a second design.
