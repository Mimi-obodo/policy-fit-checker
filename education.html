Insurance, read properly

The catalog only makes sense if the underlying products do. Here is how real insurers approach each major type of cover, paraphrased from how the market works — the actual policies PFC matches against are the live catalog in the pipeline, not this page.

Life insurance

Life cover pays a lump sum if the insured person dies during the term. Insurers such as Aviva, Legal & General and Zurich typically offer level term cover (the sum stays flat) and decreasing term (commonly mortgage-linked), and almost all exclude suicide within roughly the first 12–13 months of the policy. Premiums rise with age and health risk; a waiting period before a claim is normal.

Health insurance

Private medical insurance pays for private hospital treatment and, on richer plans, day-to-day care like GP visits and dental. In Ireland the big names are Vhi, Laya and Irish Life Health; in the UK, Bupa is a common example. Nearly every health plan applies waiting periods for new or pre-existing conditions, and some insurers now include mental-health support packages rather than excluding them outright.

Income protection

Income protection replaces part of your salary if illness or injury stops you working. Insurers such as Aviva, Royal London and Legal & General use a deferred (waiting) period, commonly between 4 and 13 weeks, before payments start. The definition matters enormously: "own-occupation" pays if you cannot do your own job, while "any-occupation" only pays if you cannot do any job.

Home & renters insurance

Buildings cover protects the structure, contents cover protects what is inside it, and you can usually buy either separately. Insurers like AXA, Zurich and Aviva commonly exclude wear and tear and policies often go unoccupied for more than 60 days. Renters rarely need buildings cover, which is why the catalog carries contents-only options.

Travel insurance

Travel cover splits into single-trip and annual multi-trip, with medical bills, cancellation and baggage. Providers such as Allianz and AXA routinely exclude travel against government advisories and typically ask you to declare pre-existing conditions rather than quietly rejecting them. Medical cover limits on cheaper plans can be dramatically lower than on premium ones.

Auto insurance

Motor cover runs from third-party only up to fully comprehensive, with optional extras like breakdown cover and no-claims protection. Insurers such as Aviva, AXA and Allianz treat driving under the influence and racing or track-day use as voiding claims, and many now offer telematics (black-box) discounts to younger drivers.

Critical illness

Critical illness pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a listed condition, typically 40 or more. Most policies apply a survival period (commonly around 27 days) and exclude conditions diagnosed before the policy started. Because it pays on diagnosis rather than on death, it is often paired with life cover rather than replacing it.

Pet insurance

Pet cover pays vets' bills, usually with a choice between lifetime cover, 12-month time-limited cover, and accident-only. The market-wide catch is that pre-existing conditions and routine or preventative care are almost always excluded, and lifetime plans cap what they pay per condition each year.

Student cover

Student products bundle the things young renters and travellers actually need: contents cover for shared accommodation, low-cost health cover for under-26s, and study-abroad travel add-ons. The honest catch is that theft without forced entry and undeclared high-value items are typically excluded, which matters in shared houses.

Business liability

Business liability covers claims against a company, including employers' liability (statutory in most places), public liability, and professional indemnity. Insurers such as Allianz and Aviva tend to exclude contractual liability beyond common law and deliberate acts, and cyber liability is now commonly sold as an add-on.
